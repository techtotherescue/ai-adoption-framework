# AI Adoption Framework v8: what changes

A draft for the framework's authors, Jacek Siadkowski and Radosław Taraszka (Tech To The Rescue), written by Radosław Taraszka between 23 October and 5 November 2026. Nothing here is in force: the released version is v7 (7.0.0).

The proposed text is [`v8-draft.md`](v8-draft.md). It starts as a byte-for-byte copy of `v7.md` (7.0.0), so every change shows against v7. This note says what changes and why. The decisions behind it, and every review comment, are in [`v8-feedback.md`](v8-feedback.md). The domain proposed for each condition v7 does not place is in [`v8-domains.md`](v8-domains.md).

## Summary

v8 keeps the five stages, the seven capacity domains, the Adoption Loop and the words of v7's conditions. It asks an organization nothing new. It changes three things:

1. **How a stage is reached.** Every condition either decides a stage or is a readiness condition. A stage is confirmed, conditional or not reached, so the readiness checklist counts.
2. **Where conditions belong.** Every condition belongs to one domain, except those that say how far AI goes. The fragility flags become conditions, and the gates for the people served become Stage 3 readiness conditions.
3. **What a result says.** It has three parts. Disagreement between the people who answered has a consequence. The recommended actions follow the support available now, and the result names its framework version.

Some rules change, so an organization's result can change. That makes v8 a major release, with a crosswalk from the v7 identifiers.

## Changes that can move an organization's result

### Readiness counts: confirmed, conditional, not reached (§2, §5)

In v7 a readiness checklist "never lowers the stage" (§2). A few conditions place the stage, and the rest of the profile cannot change it. In v8:

| Status | When |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed | The stage line and every deciding and readiness condition are met, and every stage below is confirmed. |
| Conditional | The stage line and every deciding condition are met, more than half of the readiness conditions that apply are met, and every stage below is confirmed. |
| Not reached | Anything else. |

The organization's stage is its highest confirmed stage, or the stage just above it if that one is conditional. Only one stage can be conditional. Stage 2 has no readiness list and is never conditional. A condition that applies only in some cases says so ("Where …"), and where it does not apply it counts neither for the stage nor against it. A conditional stage is assessed again at the next assessment; there are no deadlines. A condition about a workflow is met only when every workflow it applies to meets it; Stage 4 needs only one workflow that meets all its conditions, as in v7.

Why: with v7's rule, a handful of answers decide the stage and the rest of the assessment only describes it. A conditional stage says the organization has reached a stage but does not yet stand on it firmly. The cost is a stricter ladder: Stage 4 is assessed only once Stage 3 is confirmed, so one unmet Stage 3 item keeps a working Stage 4 workflow from counting. The result then says in one sentence that AI goes further than the stage.

### Stage 2 is reached by using AI (§2, §5)

v7 reaches Stage 2 when people use AI in their own work or when someone owns its adoption. In v8 only use counts, and Stage 1 reads "The organization does not use AI in its work". Stage 1 has no conditions: an organization is there whenever Stage 2 is not reached. An appointed owner counts in Leadership, where a named owner with time and authority decides Stage 3.

Why: the stage line says how far AI goes, and an owner is a capacity. The case is rare: an organization has to appoint an owner while nobody uses AI. Such an organization moves from Stage 2 to Stage 1.

### The gates for the people served move to Stage 3 (§5)

In v7 the seven gates never lower the stage; a missing one makes its domain red at any stage. In v8 they are Stage 3 readiness conditions, each worded "Where AI affects the people served: …". A missing gate keeps Stage 3 from being confirmed and comes first among the recommended actions.

AI affects the people served when the people who feel a mistake first are the people served, people deciding on access to a service, or the wider public; or when a workflow in or going into normal operations touches them or decides something about a specific person. When nobody knows whether a workflow touches them or decides about a person, it counts as doing so. Sensitive data or a severe worst consequence do not switch the gates on by themselves; where they contradict the answers above, the result shows the contradiction.

Why: v8 has two kinds of condition and nothing beside them, so the gates had to become conditions of some stage. Stage 3 has the first readiness list, and pilots there can already reach the people served. A gate is one of 24 Stage 3 readiness conditions, so an organization missing one can still hold Stage 3 as conditional; it cannot confirm it.

### Fragility flags become conditions (§5, §7)

v7 lists 23 flags beside the domains, and many repeat a checklist item, so one gap shows twice. In v8, 17 merge into the condition they repeat, and 6 become readiness conditions of their own: four at Stage 3 (a successor for the AI owner; the people who do the work take part in shaping how AI is used; pro-bono work with an independent check and a handover; results judged by outcomes) and two at Stage 4 (a review of failures after deployment; a plan for when temporary funding ends). Each is met where its flag would not be raised. Where each flag went is in the table below.

### Several respondents (§2)

v7 says nothing about an assessment answered by several people. In v8:

- An answer that claims progress takes the majority; with an even number, the more cautious of the two middle answers.
- An answer that sets the bar takes the most demanding: who feels a mistake first, the worst consequence, whether a workflow decides something about a specific person.
- Whether a week's failure would harm the people served or stop a core operation claims progress, because it puts a workflow on the Stage 4 line, so it takes the majority.
- "We don't know" does not vote. Where several answers can be ticked, every answer anyone ticked counts.
- Each readiness list from Stage 3 includes the team's agreement: "Where this is the highest stage whose deciding conditions are met: at least two people answered, and they agree on the deciding answers of this stage and of the stages below." It applies only at the highest stage whose deciding conditions are met, so certainty is checked once, where the organization stands. While people disagree on a deciding answer up to that stage, or when one person answered, that stage is at most conditional; the stages below it are confirmed by their own conditions. Stage 2 has no readiness list, so it has no agreement condition, and one person's answer is enough for it.
- Every difference is shown in the part of the result it belongs to.

Why: a stage that one person's word can raise is not certain, and a team that disagrees about where it stands has to settle that first.

## Changes to how a result is presented

### A result in three parts (§9, §10)

v7 lists nine things an assessment returns (§10). v8 groups them by the question each answers:

| Part | Question |
| --- | --- |
| AI adoption aspiration | What kind of organization is this, and how far is it sensible to take AI? |
| AI adoption stage | Where is the organization today? |
| Recommendations for further AI adoption | What should it do first, and who can help? |

The aspiration never changes the stage; it sets the bar. The project-readiness note (§9) becomes the sentence the stage part prints when AI goes further than the stage.

### The stage line (§2, §6)

Six deciding conditions say how far AI goes: people use AI in their own work (Stage 2); pilots run on selected workflows, and AI use is expected in selected areas (Stage 3); AI is integral to a workflow that matters to the mission, and that workflow runs in normal operations (Stage 4); at least two independent organizations use what the organization created (Stage 5). They belong to no domain and form the stage line. Every other condition belongs to one domain.

Why: v7 already treats depth of use as evidence for the stage (§6). As an eighth domain the stage line would repeat the stage ladder, and an organization that rightly stays at Stage 3 would show a gap in it for good.

### In place or missing, gaps and recommended actions (§6)

v7 colours a domain green, yellow or red, and leaves yellow and green to the assessor (its open question 7). v8 drops the colours: a domain shows which of its conditions are in place and which are missing toward the next stage. The next stage is the one the organization is working toward: its conditional stage, or else the stage above its highest confirmed stage, so Stage 3 for an organization at Stage 2. The framework does not say how an assessment tool counts or displays these conditions (RT, 5 November). Every missing condition is a gap, and at most three become recommended actions. v8 does not use "binding constraint", because the order below is not only what holds the organization back most.

### Recommendations follow the support available now (§8)

v7 names at most three binding constraints, gaps in the gates for the people served first, with a support pathway for each. In v8 the recommendations start with what the people who answered must agree on (with one respondent, ask at least one more person to answer). This comes before the actions and is not one of them. The actions then come in this order:

1. Gaps in the gates for the people served, with or without support.
2. Gaps that support available to the organization now can help with: what a workflow in operation lacks, then the rest of the conditional stage, then the deciding conditions of the stage above.
3. The other gaps, which stay listed. Support whose call nobody has checked counts as not available; it may still be named among the gaps, marked as unchecked.

The recommendations are worked out again whenever the result is read, so they follow support that opens and closes; the diagnosis stays as issued. With a workflow in operation, the next Adoption Loop step is the step of the first recommended gap. When the stage above fails only on its stage line, the recommendation is the next step on the line, such as running a pilot on one selected workflow.

### Date and framework version (§10)

A result shows the date it was issued and the framework version it was issued on. It does not expire. When a later version is released, it says it was issued on an earlier one, since the version is what goes out of date. A later assessment shows what changed since the previous one.

## What stays as in v7

- **Two conditions that span two domains stay whole:** Stage 4's operating practices (S4.def.6), in Governance, and Stage 5's sustainable funding with maintenance and incident capacity, in Resources. Split by domain, one missing practice would count as two gaps.
- **No Stage 3 risk screen.** v7's Governance indicator for Stage 3 names basic risk screening, but its Stage 3 checklist has no item for it. v8 keeps it that way; the flag on unassessed risks merges into the Stage 4 risk and consequence assessment.
- **The words of every condition**, and the questions in the placement tables, with two exceptions. Stage 1 and Stage 2 no longer mention who owns AI adoption, in their placement or their question. Each of the seven gates for the people served starts with "Where AI affects the people served:".
- **On hold:** asking, for each answer that decides a stage, what is in place, since when, and who is responsible. It needs new questions.

## An example

Northgate Food Bank, invented, distributes food parcels. For a year its AI has ranked the families who apply, as part of normal operations. Nobody owns the data the ranking uses, families cannot question the ranking, criteria for which workflows are ready for normal operations are missing, there is no baseline, and nobody is arranged to take over from the AI owner. Three people answered: two say the team shares what works regularly, one says informally.

Under v7, Northgate is at Stage 3. Leadership, Data, Governance and Evidence are red. Its binding constraints are the appeal route, the data owner and the workflow criteria. The disagreement is listed and changes nothing.

Under v8:

- **Aspiration:** a hybrid organization, healthy aim Stage 3 or 4, high risk. AI decides about specific families, so the gates for the people served apply.
- **Stage:** Stage 3, conditional. The conditions that decide Stage 3 are met, and 19 of its 24 readiness conditions. The result says that AI already runs a workflow at Stage 4. Stage 4 is not assessed while Stage 3 is conditional, and it would also need a data owner.
- **Recommendations:** agree on how the team shares what works; then a way for families to question the ranking; an owner for the data; criteria for normal operations. The missing successor stays listed, marked as having no support available yet.
