# AI Adoption Framework v7: what changes

A draft by the framework's authors, Jacek Siadkowski and Radosław Taraszka (Tech To The Rescue). First draft 28 September 2026; revised 30 September and 5 October 2026 by Radosław Taraszka. Jacek Siadkowski has not reviewed the revisions. Nothing here is in force: the released version is v6 (6.0.1).

The proposed text is [`v7-draft.md`](v7-draft.md). It starts as a byte-for-byte copy of `v6.md` (6.0.1), so every change shows against v6. This note says what changes and why. Review comments and the decisions taken on them are in [`v7-feedback.md`](v7-feedback.md).

## How to review

- **Files changed** on the pull request compares `v7-draft.md` with v6. "Display the rich diff" shows it rendered.
- **Commit by commit:** each commit makes one change. In "Restructure the stage profiles" no v6 sentence changes its words; it moves text, renames headings, and rewrites the Placement blocks of the previous commit as lists.
- **Side by side:** [`v7-compare.html`](v7-compare.html) puts v6 on the left and the v7 draft on the right. Download it and open it in a browser. It is regenerated with every revision of the draft; moved text shows as removed on one side and added on the other.

## Summary

v7 keeps the five stages, the seven capacity domains, the colours and the Adoption Loop. It changes three things:

1. Every stage says what places an organization there, what it requires, and what is only a readiness checklist. v6 said this in part for Stages 3 and 4, with a different kind of list for each stage.
2. Some rules change, so an organization's result can change. That makes v7 a major release, with a crosswalk from the v6 identifiers.
3. The text is reorganized for reading. The conditions keep their words.

## Changes that can move an organization's result

### Stage placement (§2, §5)

| Stage | Reached when | Required | What v6 said |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Resisting | The organization does not use AI in its work, and nobody owns its adoption. | Nothing. | A description of attitudes: skepticism, concerns, AI seen as a threat. |
| 2 Exploring | People use AI in their own work, however unevenly. | Nothing more. | A description, with no rule. |
| 3 Systematizing | The organization runs AI pilots on selected workflows, or has run the Adoption Loop on them more than once. | A named AI owner, champion, or adoption group with time and authority; people share what works and what fails. | "Most of" thirteen completion signals, the owner being one of them. |
| 4 Innovating | AI is integral to a workflow that matters to mission delivery or organizational performance. | The rest of what defines Stage 4; accountable AI adoption and technical ownership; data/context ownership and quality controls. | Seven defining conditions and fifteen operating gates the organization "should have", without saying which are required. |
| 5 Enabling | Other organizations depend on the organization's AI capability and obtain demonstrated value from it. | What Stage 4 requires; sustainable funding; maintenance and incident capacity. | Twelve guardrails, all required. |

An organization is at the highest stage it reaches whose requirements it meets, together with the requirements of every stage below. Everything else in a stage profile is a readiness checklist: an unmet item is a gap to close and never lowers the stage.

Why each stage changes:

- **Stage 1** is placed by what the organization does, because an assessment cannot establish attitudes reliably. Skepticism and concerns stay in the profile as typical traits.
- **Stage 3** requires the owner, champion, or adoption group named in v6's own definition. Its completion signals listed an owner as one of thirteen and asked for "most" of them, so an organization could reach Stage 3 with nobody leading adoption. It also requires that people share what works and what fails, which v6 lists among the conditions for moving to Stage 3 ("champions can demonstrate wins and failures", §10).
- **Stage 4** is decided by its definition and two of its gates. v6 did not say whether all fifteen gates were required. A workflow matters to the mission when it passes a test: if it failed for a week, the people served would be harmed, or a core operation would stop.
- **Stage 5** keeps as requirements the guardrails that protect the organizations depending on the capability: sustainable funding, and maintenance and incident capacity. The other nine become a readiness checklist. This relaxes v6, which required all twelve. Most of the nine, such as boundaries on corporate influence or credibility with affected communities, need an expert's judgment rather than a fact an assessment can check.
- **The gates for beneficiary or external exposure** never lower the stage. When AI affects the people the organization serves, a missing gate makes its domain red.

### Red means a required condition is missing (§6)

v6 defines green, yellow and red but does not say how to decide between them. v7 adds a rule for red: a domain is red when it is missing a condition the organization's next step requires. Each such condition belongs to one domain; the proposed assignment is in [`v7-domains.md`](v7-domains.md). The assessment names at most three binding constraints, gaps in the beneficiary gates first. The line between yellow and green stays the assessor's judgment.

A small organization at Stage 2 that drafts its newsletter with AI can be red in Technology for lacking a view of its dependence on vendors and partners, an item of the Stage 3 checklist. It is not red for lacking a plan to leave its AI provider, a Stage 4 gate, because it runs no AI workflow in operation.

### Social impact organizations, not only nonprofits (title, §1, §6, §9, §12)

The framework is already used to assess social enterprises and impact ventures. v7 says so: the title becomes "AI Adoption Framework for Social Impact Organizations", and §1 defines social impact organizations as nonprofits, social enterprises, and impact ventures. §8 keeps "another nonprofit intermediary", which names a kind of support provider.

The _Resources and sustainability_ question gains one sentence: for an organization that earns revenue, whether that revenue covers the costs it is meant to cover. An invented social cooperative runs a café that prices meals below cost on purpose, because a grant pays for employing people with disabilities. Its revenue covers what it is meant to cover, so the planned subsidy does not count against it.

## Changes to the text only

- **Stage profiles (§5)** have three parts: Placement, What it looks like, and What helps. "What Defines This Stage" is split between what places the organization, what it requires, and typical traits. The completion signals, operating gates and guardrails are each called the readiness checklist. Labels in the text replace most sub-headings.
- **Observable indicators** move from each stage to one table per domain in §6, so each domain reads across the five stages in one place.
- **§11, TTTR Program Implications, is removed.** It mapped the stages to Tech To The Rescue's own programs; §8 already describes support by type, from any provider. Tech To The Rescue keeps the mapping in its own materials, and v6 stays published whole. §12 and §13 become §11 and §12.
- "Version 6" and "v6" become "Version 7" and "v7", and the header marks the text as a draft.

## Not yet in this draft

- §10, Recommended Stage Transitions, is to be removed: its lists repeat the stage profiles (decision P6 in the feedback register).
- Passages that contradict the changes: §9 still asks for "the full operating gates" at Stage 4; §6 and §11 still say "two or three" binding constraints; §6 still names the "completion signals" and "operating gates".
- Three Stage 4 checklist items repeat requirements in other words: a redesigned workflow and role model; human review, escalation, exception, and appeal rules; maintenance and incident ownership.
- Each stage's "Adoption Loop focus" repeats the list at the end of §4.
- Stage 1 still lists "Little direct use inside the organization" among its typical traits, while what places an organization at Stage 1 is that it does not use AI. An organization with a little use is at Stage 2.
- A style pass over the prose.

## Open questions

1. Do these changes keep what v6 intends?
2. Is the wording of what places an organization at Stage 3 right? No decision is behind it yet.
3. [`v7-domains.md`](v7-domains.md) marks seventeen conditions whose domain is disputed. Is the proposed domain right for each?
4. How should an assessor tell yellow from green? v7 leaves this to the assessor's judgment; we propose settling it in v8.

## An example

Take an invented food bank that uses AI to decide who gets an emergency food parcel. It has rules for AI and monitors it. A named person is accountable for mistakes, another keeps its eligibility rules and family records up to date, and staff check every refusal and share what they learn across teams. But a family the AI refuses cannot find out why, and has no one to ask to look again. The food bank also has no plan for losing its AI provider.

Under v6, nobody can say what colour its _Governance, risk, and accountability_ domain should be, or whether it is at Stage 3 or Stage 4.

Under v7:

- The food bank is at Stage 4. Allocating food parcels passes the test of a workflow the mission depends on, and the food bank meets what Stage 4 requires.
- Its governance domain is red. AI shapes a decision about each family, so the family needs a way to appeal. This is the food bank's first binding constraint.
- Its technology domain is red. The missing plan for losing its provider is a gap to close in a workflow it already operates. It does not lower the stage.

If nobody at the food bank had the time or authority to lead its AI adoption, it would be at Stage 2.
