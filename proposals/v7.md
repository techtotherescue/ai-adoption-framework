# AI Adoption Framework v7: proposed changes

A draft by the framework's authors, Jacek Siadkowski and Radosław Taraszka (Tech To The Rescue), for anyone who uses the framework or wants to. First draft 28 September 2026; revised 30 September 2026 by Radosław Taraszka for Jacek Siadkowski's review. Nothing here is in force yet.

We propose five changes to v6. The stages, the capacity domains, the colours and the Adoption Loop stay. What changes is how an organization is placed on a stage and how a domain turns red, so an organization's result can change. That makes v7 a major release, with a crosswalk from the v6 identifiers.

## An example

Take an invented food bank that uses AI to decide who gets an emergency food parcel. It has rules for AI and monitors it. A named person is accountable for mistakes, another keeps its eligibility rules and family records up to date, and staff check every refusal. But a family the AI refuses cannot find out why, and has no one to ask to look again. The food bank also has no plan for losing its AI provider.

Under v6, nobody can say what colour its _Governance, risk, and accountability_ domain should be, or whether it is at Stage 3 or Stage 4. The fifteen Stage 4 operating gates include a plan for losing a provider, and v6 does not say whether all fifteen are required.

Under v7:

- The food bank is at Stage 4. Allocating food parcels is a workflow the mission depends on, and the food bank meets what defines Stage 4.
- Its governance domain is red. AI shapes a decision about each family, so the family needs a way to appeal. This is the food bank's first binding constraint.
- Its technology domain is red. The missing plan for losing its provider is a gap to close in a workflow it already operates. It does not lower the stage.

Now suppose nobody at the food bank had the time or authority to lead its AI adoption. Under v6 it could still be at Stage 3. Under v7 it would be at Stage 2.

## 1. Red means a required condition is missing

v6 defines green, yellow and red, but does not say how to decide between them. We propose a rule for red. Each condition that can make a domain red belongs to exactly one domain, recorded in `v7.json`. The proposed assignment is in [`v7-domains.md`](v7-domains.md).

Proposed addition to §6, How To Read The Domains, after the three colours:

> A domain is red when it is missing a condition that the organization's next step requires. The required conditions are:
>
> - the Stage 3 completion signals, for an organization at Stage 2 or Stage 3;
> - the Stage 4 operating gates, for each workflow the organization is putting into operation or already operates;
> - the additional gates for beneficiary or external exposure, whenever AI affects the people the organization serves, at any stage;
> - the absence of every fragility flag: a flag that is present counts as a missing condition.
>
> Each condition belongs to one domain. A red domain does not lower the organization's stage. Each missing condition is a binding constraint. The assessment names at most three, and gaps in the additional gates for beneficiary or external exposure come first. The line between yellow and green remains the assessor's judgment.

A small organization at Stage 2 that drafts its newsletter with AI can be red in Technology for lacking a view of its dependence on vendors and partners, which is a Stage 3 completion signal. It is not red for lacking a plan to leave its AI provider, which is a Stage 4 gate, because it runs no AI workflow in operation. A Stage 2 organization may be missing many signals at once, which is why the assessment names at most three.

## 2. Stage 3 requires someone to lead adoption

v6 defines Stage 3 with "a named AI owner, champion, or adoption group" that "has time and authority". Its completion signals list an owner as one of thirteen and ask for "most" of them, so an organization can reach Stage 3 without anyone leading adoption. We propose that the definition decides.

Proposed change to §5, Stage 3 Completion Signals:

> A solid Stage 3 organization should have most of the following. It must have a named AI owner, champion, or adoption group with time and authority. Without one, it cannot be placed above Stage 2.

An organization that leads adoption through a named group rather than one person still meets this.

## 3. Stage 4 is decided by its definition

v6 describes Stage 4 twice: seven conditions under What Defines This Stage, and fifteen operating gates an organization "should have". It does not say whether all fifteen gates are required. We propose that the definition, together with two of the gates, decides the stage, and the other gates are a readiness checklist.

Proposed addition under §5, Stage 4 Operating Gates:

> Stage 4 requires what defines this stage (beneficiary-facing deployment remains optional), accountable AI adoption and technical ownership, and data/context ownership and quality controls. The other gates are a readiness checklist. An unmet gate is a gap to close (§6) and does not lower the stage.
>
> "Governance, maintenance, dependency, and failure handling are active operating practices" describes the organization's practice. It is not met or failed by whether every workflow meets every gate.
>
> A workflow matters to mission delivery or organizational performance when it passes this test: if it failed or went wrong for a week, the people the organization serves would be harmed, or a core operation would stop. Allocating emergency food parcels passes. Drafting a newsletter does not. Writing grant applications passes only when the organization depends on those grants and AI is integral to how they are written.

Proposed addition under §5, Additional Gates For Beneficiary Or External Exposure:

> These gates never lower the stage. When AI affects the people the organization serves, a missing gate makes its domain red (§6). An appeal route is relevant when AI makes or shapes a decision about a specific person: who receives help, who is refused, or in what order people are served.

## 4. Social impact organizations, not only nonprofits

The framework is already used to assess social enterprises and impact ventures as well as nonprofits. We propose that it says so.

- Title: "AI Adoption Framework for Social Impact Organizations".
- §1, The Core Thesis, and §13, Core Message: "Nonprofit AI adoption" becomes "AI adoption in social impact organizations", for example "**AI adoption in social impact organizations is an operating-model transition.**" §1 adds: "Social impact organizations are nonprofits, social enterprises, and impact ventures."
- §9: "Many nonprofits will deploy AI" becomes "Many social impact organizations will deploy AI".
- §8 keeps "another nonprofit intermediary", which names a kind of support provider.

The _Resources and sustainability_ question in §6 gains one sentence:

> For an organization that earns revenue, this includes whether that revenue covers the costs it is meant to cover.

An invented social cooperative runs a café that prices meals below cost on purpose, because a grant pays for employing people with disabilities. Its revenue covers what it is meant to cover, so the question does not count the planned subsidy against it.

## 5. The framework does not name one provider's programs

v6 §11, TTTR Program Implications, maps the stages to Tech To The Rescue's own programs: Readiness / Pre-Lab, Impact Lab, Scaling Program, and a Stage 5 / Infrastructure Track. §8 already describes support by type, from any provider. We propose removing §11 from v7. Tech To The Rescue keeps the program mapping in its own materials, and v6 stays published whole.

§12 and §13 become §11 and §12. No condition identifier is affected: §11 has none.

## Editorial changes

These change no result:

- "Version 6" and "v6" in the text become "Version 7" and "v7", including "Core v6 Message".
- "Must" and "requires" decide a stage or a colour; "should" describes good practice.
- One term per idea: "gap to close" for an unmet gate, and the wording of What Defines This Stage for the person or group who leads adoption.
- The rules cite conditions by their text; `v7.json` records them by identifier.

## Open questions

1. Do these five changes keep what v6 intends?
2. Should the Stage 3 completion signals stay required after an organization reaches Stage 4?
3. [`v7-domains.md`](v7-domains.md) marks seventeen conditions whose domain is disputed. Is the proposed domain right for each?
4. How should an assessor tell yellow from green? v7 leaves this to the assessor's judgment; we propose settling it in v8.

## Changes from the 28 September draft

- Change 1 now says which conditions are required, assigns each to one domain, keeps at most three binding constraints, and leaves yellow and green to v8.
- Change 2 requires the owner, champion, or adoption group from What Defines This Stage, not the narrower completion signal.
- Change 3 requires data/context ownership and quality controls in place of "maintained, governed context (§10)", excludes the optional beneficiary-facing line, and answers the draft's open question about which workflows the mission depends on. It also says the beneficiary gates never lower the stage.
- Change 4 covers the thesis, the core message and §9, not only the title, and the revenue sentence no longer penalizes planned subsidies.
- Change 5 is new.
