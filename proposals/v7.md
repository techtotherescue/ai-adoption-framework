# AI Adoption Framework v7: proposed changes

A draft by the framework's authors, Jacek Siadkowski and Radosław Taraszka (Tech To The Rescue), for anyone who uses the framework or wants to. 28 September 2026. Nothing here is in force yet.

We propose four changes to v6. Each one settles something v6 leaves open, and none of them changes the stages, the capacity domains, the colours or the Adoption Loop. Changes to the AI Adoption Navigator, the tool that runs the assessment, are proposed separately.

## An example

Take an invented food bank that uses AI to decide who gets an emergency food parcel. It has rules for AI and monitors it. A named person is accountable for mistakes, and staff check every refusal. But a family the AI refuses cannot find out why, and has no one to ask to look again.

Under v6, nobody can say what colour its *Governance, risk, and accountability* domain should be. The domain is strong with one gap, and v6 does not say how to judge that. Under v7 the domain is red, because AI that decides about people needs an appeal route.

The food bank has no plan for losing its AI provider, which is one of the fifteen Stage 4 operating gates. If all fifteen are required, v6 places it at Stage 3. Under v7 it is at Stage 4, and the missing plan is a gap to close.

Now suppose its AI lead had no time or authority. Under v6 it could still be at Stage 3. Under v7 it would be at Stage 2.

## 1. Red means a required condition is missing

v6 defines green, yellow and red, but does not say how to decide between them. We propose a rule for red only. The definitions of green and yellow stay as they are. The conditions come from v6's own checklists: the Stage 3 completion signals, the Stage 4 operating gates, the additional gates for AI that affects the people an organization serves, and the fragility flags.

Proposed addition to §6, How To Read The Domains:

> A domain is judged against named conditions, each met or not, set by the organization's risk and scope. A domain missing a required condition is red, and the missing condition is its binding constraint. A red domain does not lower the organization's stage.

For the food bank, the missing appeal route makes governance red and becomes its binding constraint. The food bank stays at Stage 4.

## 2. Stage 3 requires an AI owner

v6 defines Stage 3 with an owner who has "time and authority". Its completion signals list that owner as one of thirteen and ask for "most" of them, so an organization can reach Stage 3 without one. We propose that the definition decides.

Proposed change to §5, Stage 3 Completion Signals:

> A solid Stage 3 organization should have most of the following. The first, an AI adoption owner with time and authority, is required.

A food bank whose AI lead has no time or authority would be at Stage 2.

## 3. Stage 4 does not require all fifteen gates

v6 describes Stage 4 twice: seven conditions under What Defines This Stage, and fifteen operating gates an organization "should have". It does not say whether all fifteen gates are required. We propose that the definition decides the stage and the other gates are tasks.

Proposed addition under §5, Stage 4 Operating Gates:

> Stage 4 requires the conditions under What Defines This Stage, accountable adoption and technical ownership, and maintained, governed context (§10). The other gates are a readiness checklist. An unmet gate is a gap to close and does not lower the stage.

The Navigator already works this way. Its methodology lists which questions decide each stage and which are tasks, under [Stage placement](https://wef-demo.vercel.app/methodology#stage-placement).

The food bank's missing plan for losing its AI provider is a task, so the food bank stays at Stage 4.

## 4. Social impact organizations, not only nonprofits

The Navigator assesses social enterprises and impact ventures as well as nonprofits. We propose that the title become "for Social Impact Organizations", and that the *Resources and sustainability* question in §6 gain one sentence:

> For an organization that earns revenue, this includes whether its prices cover its costs.

## Open questions

1. Do these four changes keep what v6 intends?
2. Stage 4 rests on workflows "the mission depends on". How should an assessor decide which workflows those are?
3. Change 1 gives a rule for red only. How should an assessor tell yellow from green?
